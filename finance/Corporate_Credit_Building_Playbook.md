## Executive Summary

This playbook outlines how to establish legitimate corporate credit for Sentinel Labs, achieving an 800+ Dun & Bradstreet (D&B) score within 90–180 days using zero personal credit and minimal cash outlay.

**Core mechanism:** Business credit is separate from personal credit. By establishing a business entity, securing a business bank account, building trade lines with vendors, and creating a positive payment history, you build institutional creditworthiness that lenders and vendors recognize.

**Timeline:** 
- Days 1-30: Foundation setup (EIN, bank account, D&B registration)
- Days 31-90: Trade line establishment (vendor relationships, net terms)
- Days 91-180: Credit maturation (consistent payment history, score optimization)

**Cost:** $0–$2,000 total (mostly vendor deposits, business registration, optional credit monitoring)

**Outcome:** 800+ D&B score, business credit lines, vendor relationships, lending readiness.

---

## Why Corporate Credit Matters

**Corporate credit ≠ personal credit.**

Most founders rely on personal credit (FICO score) and personal guarantees to secure business credit. This is expensive and risky—one missed payment tanks your personal credit, affecting mortgage rates, insurance, and more.

**Legitimate corporate credit separates personal and business finances.** Lenders and vendors see your business as creditworthy on its own merits, based on:
- Business credit history (payment patterns)
- D&B business profile (size, stability, industry)
- Trade line relationships (vendor payment history)
- Banking relationships (account longevity, transaction patterns)

**Strategic advantages:**
- **Founder credit protection** — Business problems don't destroy personal credit
- **Lending access** — Business credit lines available at competitive rates
- **Vendor leverage** — Net-30/60/90 terms instead of cash upfront
- **Fundraising credibility** — Investors see institutional stability
- **Acquisition readiness** — SBA loans, vendor financing, equipment leasing available

## Foundation Phase (Days 1–30)

### Step 1: Establish Legal Entity (Days 1–5)

**Action:** Form a Delaware C-Corporation or LLC for Sentinel Labs.

**Why Delaware?**
- Corporate-friendly jurisdiction (precedent, investor preference)
- Separate liability protection
- Tax flexibility (C-Corp for reinvestment strategy, LLC for pass-through)
- Professional credibility signal

**How:**
- Online service: Stripe Atlas, LawFirm.com, or Delaware Division of Corporations
- Cost: $150–$300
- Timeline: 1–3 business days

**What you get:**
- Certificate of incorporation/articles of organization
- EIN (Employer Identification Number) — IRS assigns automatically
- Legal separation of business and personal liabilities

**Action items:**
- [ ] Register business entity
- [ ] Receive EIN confirmation from IRS (check email/mail within 1 week)
- [ ] Store certificates in secure location

---

### Step 2: Obtain EIN & Verify with IRS (Days 3–7)

**Action:** Confirm your EIN is active with the IRS.

**How:**
- EIN auto-assigns when you register with Delaware (if filed correctly)
- Verify at IRS.gov using your business name + EIN
- Timeline: 1–7 business days from registration

**Why this matters:**
- D&B needs verified EIN to assign DUNS number
- Vendors need EIN for W-9 verification
- Banks need EIN for business account verification

**Action items:**
- [ ] Receive EIN confirmation
- [ ] Verify EIN at IRS.gov
- [ ] Record EIN in company records

### Step 3: Open Business Bank Account (Days 8–20)

**Action:** Open a dedicated business bank account in the company's name.

**Why separate account?**
- Creates transaction history (banks report to D&B)
- Establishes banking relationship (credit consideration)
- Separates personal and business finances (audit trail, tax clarity)
- Shows business stability (consistent account activity)

**Where to open:**
- **Best for credit building:** Traditional banks (Wells Fargo, Bank of America, Chase)
  - Report account history to D&B and credit bureaus
  - Establish banking relationship (matters for future lending)
  - Higher account minimums but stronger credit signal

- **Good for speed:** Online banks (Mercury, Brex, Wise)
  - Faster opening (same day)
  - Lower minimums
  - May not report to D&B (less credit benefit)

**Recommendation:** Open at both.
1. Traditional bank (credit building)
2. Online bank (operational convenience)

**What you need:**
- Business registration documents (certificate of incorporation/organization)
- EIN confirmation letter
- Photo ID (founder)
- Personal SSN (for background check)

**Cost:** $0 (most banks waive minimum for business accounts)

**Action items:**
- [ ] Choose primary bank (traditional)
- [ ] Choose secondary bank (operational)
- [ ] Submit applications
- [ ] Receive debit cards and account confirmations
- [ ] Record account numbers and routing numbers
- [ ] Set up online banking access

### Step 4: Register with Dun & Bradstreet (Days 10–15)

**Action:** Get your DUNS number from Dun & Bradstreet.

**What is DUNS?**
- Dun & Bradstreet Universal Numbering System
- Business identifier (like a personal SSN)
- Required for corporate credit scoring
- Free to register

**How:**
- Go to DandB.com
- Select "Get a DUNS Number"
- Enter business information (name, address, EIN, business type)
- Confirm phone/email
- Timeline: 1–5 business days

**What D&B captures:**
- Business name and address
- EIN and legal structure
- Industry classification (SIC code)
- Business description
- Founder/principal information

**Why this matters:**
- D&B uses DUNS + business data to calculate business credit score
- Score ranges 0–100 (higher = better creditworthiness)
- Lenders and vendors use D&B scores to make decisions

**Action items:**
- [ ] Register for DUNS number
- [ ] Receive DUNS confirmation
- [ ] Record DUNS number
- [ ] Verify D&B business profile is accurate
- [ ] Update any incorrect information (business address, phone, etc.)

**Action:** Establish accounting infrastructure to track business transactions.

**Why:**
- D&B pulls business data from credit reports, vendors, banks, and public records
- Consistent, documented financial activity builds credit profile
- Lenders will request financial statements (need clean records)

**How:**

3. **Establish payment processing**
   - Stripe, Square, or similar for customer payments
   - Creates documented revenue activity
   - Banks look at transaction volume and consistency

**Action items:**
- [ ] Set up accounting software

- [ ] Open business credit card (optional)
- [ ] Set up payment processing for customer revenue
- [ ] Generate first P&L statement (should show business activity)

## Trade Line Establishment Phase (Days 31–90)

### Step 6: Identify Strategic Vendor Relationships (Days 31–45)

**Action:** Build intentional relationships with vendors who report to D&B and credit bureaus.

**What are trade lines?**
- Credit relationships with vendors/suppliers
- Payment history (on-time = credit building)
- Reported to credit bureaus and D&B
- Example: "Net-30 terms with Vendor X, paid on time for 6 months"

**Strategic vendor types (in priority order):**

**Tier 1: High-Reporting Vendors** (report to D&B + credit bureaus)
- Office supply companies (Staples, Office Depot)
- Telecommunications (AT&T Business, Verizon Business)
- Business insurance providers
- Commercial shipping (FedEx Business, UPS)
- Software-as-a-Service providers (Adobe, Salesforce, HubSpot)

**Tier 2: Reporting Vendors** (report to D&B)
- Business utilities (electric, gas, internet)
- Commercial printing (Vistaprint, local printers)
- Industry-specific suppliers
- Professional services (accounting, legal, consulting)

**Tier 3: Relationship Builders** (less reporting, but relationship value)
- Local vendors
- Industry partners
- Service providers

**Strategy:** Establish 5–8 vendor relationships across tiers.

**How to secure vendor relationships:**

**For Tier 1 vendors (most important):**
1. Contact vendor account setup team
2. Request "Net-30" or "Net-60" terms (instead of cash upfront)
3. Provide business information:
   - Business name, address, EIN
   - DUNS number
   - D&B profile link
   - Business description
4. Expect initial small credit limit ($500–$5K)
5. Make first purchase (small)
6. Pay within net terms (don't miss deadline)

**For Tier 2 vendors:**
1. Contact vendor; request business account
2. Ask about credit terms
3. Start with cash purchases; demonstrate reliability
4. Request net terms after 2–3 months of on-time payments

**Action items:**
- [ ] Identify 8 target vendors (mix of tiers)
- [ ] Contact each vendor; request business account
- [ ] Request Net-30 or Net-60 terms
- [ ] Document vendor contact, terms, credit limits
- [ ] Complete first purchase with 3–4 vendors

### Step 7: Execute Strategic Purchases & Payment Schedule (Days 45–90)

**Action:** Make intentional purchases on vendor credit lines and establish flawless payment history.

**Why this matters:**
- D&B scores based 50%+ on payment history
- On-time payments = credit building
- Late payments = score damage (takes 6+ months to recover)

**Purchase strategy:**

**Month 1 (Days 45–60):**
- Make small purchases with 3–4 vendors ($200–$1K each)
- Spread purchases across different vendors
- Choose items you'd buy anyway (office supplies, software, services)
- Keep receipts and invoice records

**Month 2 (Days 61–90):**
- Increase purchase amounts slightly ($500–$3K each)
- Add 2–3 more vendors if possible
- Continue spreading across vendors
- Total spend: $5K–$15K (across all vendors)

**Payment execution (CRITICAL):**

**Timing rule:** Pay 7–10 days BEFORE due date.

Why? 
- Demonstrates financial health (not scrambling to pay on time)
- Ensures no accidental late payments
- Builds strongest possible payment history

**Example:**
- Invoice due date: Net-30 (e.g., Jan 30)
- Pay by: Jan 20–23
- Early payment signal: "This business pays early"

**Record keeping:**
- Document every invoice, purchase order, and payment
- Keep in accounting software
- Link payment to vendor record
- Create simple spreadsheet: Vendor | Amount | Due Date | Paid Date

**Action items:**
- [ ] Month 1: Execute first purchases with 4 vendors
- [ ] Document all invoices and payment dates
- [ ] Month 2: Scale purchases, add new vendors
- [ ] Create vendor relationship tracking spreadsheet
- [ ] Set calendar reminders: Pay each invoice 10 days early
- [ ] Maintain zero late payments

## Risk Mitigation & Guardrails

### What Could Derail Your Credit Score

**Critical (Will damage score significantly):**
1. **Missed payment** (even 1 day late)
   - Impact: -20 to -40 points on D&B score
   - Recovery: 6+ months to rebuild
   - Prevention: Set payments 10 days early; use automatic payment

2. **Collections account or lawsuit**
   - Impact: -50+ points; may reset progress
   - Prevention: Never miss a payment; maintain emergency cash

3. **Bankruptcy filing**
   - Impact: Score collapse; disqualifying for business credit
   - Prevention: Not relevant for this plan; focus on cash flow

4. **Fraudulent activity or identity theft**
   - Impact: Account suspension; investigation
   - Prevention: Monitor accounts regularly; use strong passwords

**Medium (Will damage score moderately):**
1. **High credit utilization** (>50% of available credit)
   - Impact: -10 to -15 points
   - Prevention: Keep utilization 20–40%

2. **Closed vendor account** (vendor removes credit line)
   - Impact: -5 to -10 points per line closed
   - Prevention: Maintain positive relationships; never miss payments

3. **Too many credit inquiries** (applying for too many accounts at once)
   - Impact: -5 points per hard inquiry
   - Prevention: Space vendor applications 2–4 weeks apart

**Low (Minor impact):**
1. **Occasional late payment** (1–2 days, paid quickly)
   - Impact: -2 to -5 points
   - Prevention: Same as above—pay early

2. **Vendor limit reduction** (vendor lowers credit limit)
   - Impact: -2 to -3 points
   - Prevention: Keep utilization healthy; don't miss payments

### Emergency Protocols

**If you miss a payment (1–5 days late):**
1. Pay immediately (same day)
2. Contact vendor; explain and apologize
3. Request they not report late payment (some will honor this if it's first offense)
4. Increase payment discipline (add more calendar reminders)
5. Expect minor score impact (-2 to -5 points); monitor recovery

**If vendor closes your account:**
1. Contact vendor; ask why
2. If it's a misunderstanding, request reinstatement
3. If permanent, focus on building other relationships
4. Don't worry—you have 5–8 other vendors; closing one doesn't derail plan

**If D&B has incorrect information:**
1. Correct immediately via D&B website
2. Attach supporting documents (business registration, EIN letter, etc.)
3. D&B verifies within 2–4 business days
4. Monitor to confirm correction was applied

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a personal guarantee from the founder?**
A: Not for this credit-building plan. The goal is to build credit that stands on its own. Some lenders may request personal guarantee later, but not required for initial trade lines.

**Q: What if my business is just starting with no revenue yet?**
A: This plan works regardless. You're building credit on payment history and account management, not revenue. Many startups use this to establish credit before revenue scales.

**Q: Can I do this with an LLC instead of a C-Corp?**
A: Yes. Both work. C-Corps signal more stability to D&B; LLCs are more common for solo founders. Functionally similar for credit-building.

**Q: How much money do I need to start?**
A: Minimal. Budget $1K–$2K for business registration and first vendor deposits/payments. But technically, you can start with $0 (no deposits required for most Tier 1 vendors).

**Q: Do I need a business address, or can I use home address?**
A: Home address is fine for starting. Commercial address is nicer for professional credibility (and required by some vendors), but not essential.

**Q: What if a vendor won't extend Net-30 terms?**
A: Start with cash purchases. After 2–3 months of on-time payment, request net terms. Most will grant it if you've built relationship.

**Q: Can I use a business credit card instead of vendor trade lines?**
A: Credit card alone is weaker. D&B heavily weights trade line diversity. Business card + 5–8 vendor lines = strongest profile.

**Q: What happens if I miss one payment?**
A: One missed payment (by 1–5 days, quickly paid) causes minor damage (-2 to -5 points). But recovery is possible in 2–3 months. The key is avoiding ANY late payments during this 180-day period.

**Q: How long does credit stay strong after I achieve 800+?**
A: As long as you maintain payment discipline. Late payments damage score; on-time payments strengthen it. Credit is an ongoing habit, not a destination.

**Q: Can I use this for multiple businesses?**
A: Yes. Each business gets its own EIN, D&B registration, and credit profile. You could repeat this plan for each entity.

**Q: What about business credit agencies other than D&B?**
A: Equifax Business and Experian Business also track business credit. This plan builds all three (they share vendor payment data). Focus on D&B; the others follow.
